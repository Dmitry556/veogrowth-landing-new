Pitch reasoning

Selected play: PLAY A — incumbent displacement.

Why not lead with PLAY B: I found no opened evidence of a live fraud scheme, resale campaign, active abuse ring, or public incident naming Kylie Cosmetics in the last 1–3 months. The July 30 Mood Stones launch and public promo/subscription surfaces create exposure, but they do not prove active targeting.

Best wedge: “launch-safe approval and policy-abuse control across checkout, subscriptions, promos, and claims.” Forter is the given incumbent and appears in current/near-current public stack evidence, so the opening should be comparative without asserting renewal timing. The pitch should not say Wyllo is needed because Forter is failing. It should ask whether Forter is being evaluated only on checkout payment fraud while Kylie’s July fragrance launch and live offer/subscription mechanics create risk outside basic transaction screening.

Person hook:

  • Anna: Kylie brand growth and category expansion are on her desk. Keep this strategic: protect the launch and brand experience without adding friction.
  • Nicolas: his public posts emphasize momentum, storytelling, Skin Tint, Glossy Lip Kits, scenting expansion, Ulta recognition, and 2026 building. Hook the Mood Stones launch and launch conversion.
  • Laurent: speak in leakage/control terms: prevent avoidable loss, reduce manual review/exception cost, and defend profitable growth without over-claiming ROI.
  • Jocelyn / Guadalupe: speak to owned-site execution: notification CTA, offer stack, subscriptions, checkout/account experience, launch readiness.
  • Susan: speak to exception handling: order flags, refund/reshipment decisions, wrong-item/damaged-item claims, refused delivery, and cross-brand ecommerce operations.

What not to say:

  • Do not say Kylie has a fraud incident.
  • Do not say the Forter contract is up.
  • Do not claim Wyllo guarantees a specific approval-rate or cost improvement.
  • Do not lead with “returns fraud” because the public policy is final-sale/no returns.
  • Do not imply the public coupon pages are abuse; they are evidence of live promotional surface area.

Suggested outbound logic:

  1. Open with the dated July 30 Mood Stones launch and the public evidence that Kylie is staffing and operating around fragrance/category expansion.
  2. Acknowledge the existing fraud stack rather than pretending they have no vendor.
  3. Ask a comparative control question: whether the current setup covers launch order approvals plus promo/subscription/resale/claim-policy decisions, not just checkout fraud.
  4. Offer a review/audit angle tied to the launch and public policy surfaces.
